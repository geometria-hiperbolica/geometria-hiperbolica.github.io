Introduction

Hyperbolic geometry is one of the deepest and most surprising realizations of non-Euclidean geometry. Since Nikolai Lobachevsky, János Bolyai, and Carl Friedrich Gauss discovered in the 19th century that Euclid's fifth postulate was not a necessary consequence of the first four, geometry ceased to be a single theory of space and became a field rich in structures, models, and curvatures. In this new landscape, hyperbolic geometry emerges as the paradigm of spaces of constant negative curvature, revealing geometric and topological phenomena that have no analogue in Euclidean geometry.

A distinctive feature of hyperbolic geometry is that many intuitions inherited from Euclidean geometry fail systematically: the sum of the angles of a triangle is less than $\pi$, the hyperbolic area of a triangle is directly related to the sum of the measures of its interior angles; this fact is known as the Gauss-Bonnet Theorem. Moreover, there are infinitely many geodesics (hyperbolic lines) that pass through a point and do not intersect a given geodesic. Far from being anomalies, these phenomena reflect a deep and interesting structure, awakening interest in hyperbolic geometry.

These notes are devoted to the geometric, topological, and algebraic study of the hyperbolic plane and have arisen from the teaching of an introductory course in hyperbolic geometry, aimed at undergraduate students. Our objective is twofold: on the one hand, to develop a concrete geometric understanding of hyperbolic spaces through explicit models, such as the Poincaré disk model $\Delta$ and the upper half-plane or Lobachevsky plane $\mathbb{H}$. On the other hand, to present the different classical elements of the study of hyperbolic geometry such as: isometry group, hyperbolic Riemannian metric, geodesics, trigonometry, hyperbolic areas, Fuchsian groups, among others.

These notes assume a basic familiarity with complex geometry, analysis in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$, and topology. However, their approach will be largely geometric, analytic, and constructive. The reader will realize that we combine rigorous proofs with examples, figures, and explicit calculations, in order to develop both intuition and technique.

We hope that these notes serve not only as an introduction to the area, but also as an invitation to explore a territory where geometry and topology meet in an especially fertile way.

In some sections we have included some interactive applets to explore this fascinating branch of mathematics. This dynamic approach will allow us to visualize abstract concepts and intuitively understand the peculiarities of Hyperbolic Geometry.

Get ready for an exciting exploration of an alternative geometric universe where curiosity and experimentation will be our guides!
